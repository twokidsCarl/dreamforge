Scientific illustration in David S. Goodsell's molecular painting style (flat fills, thin dark outlines, crowded but legible, cross-section, painted texture), but in a DUSK KEY to match a dark cinematic documentary: the extracellular water is deep indigo-teal (#13203F to #1C3350) with molecules in desaturated dusty blue and violet; the membrane is warm ochre-cream lipid heads (#E3C27D) with darker amber tails; the cytoplasm below is deep plum (#2A1735) crowded with muted mauve filaments and blobs. Only story light is luminous cyan-blue (#3FA9FF) with a soft glow. No text, no labels, no watermark. A single continuous membrane slab across the frame, slight perspective. Left two-thirds: a slow multi-step relay reading left to right — (1) a violet light-sensing receptor catches a cyan photon, (2) a three-part G-protein (rose, orange, tan) detaches and drifts along the inner leaflet, (3) an intermediate enzyme (muted blue), (4) finally a teal ion channel opens far to the right; faint dotted motion trails link the steps. Right third, separated by a soft gap: a teal ion channel with a tiny bright magenta synthetic chemical photoswitch tethered to its mouth, and a few magenta molecules drifting down from above as if added from outside. Clean readable staging, not cluttered.
