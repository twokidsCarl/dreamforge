Scientific illustration in David S. Goodsell's molecular painting style (flat fills, thin dark outlines, crowded but legible, cross-section, painted texture), but in a DUSK KEY to match a dark cinematic documentary: the extracellular water is deep indigo-teal (#13203F to #1C3350) with molecules in desaturated dusty blue and violet; the membrane is warm ochre-cream lipid heads (#E3C27D) with darker amber tails; the cytoplasm below is deep plum (#2A1735) crowded with muted mauve filaments and blobs. Only story light is luminous cyan-blue (#3FA9FF) with a soft glow. No text, no labels, no watermark. Center: the light-gated ion channel channelrhodopsin-2 as a DIMER: two seven-helix subunits side by side, mint-teal (#63C7A8) with darker teal outline, packed closely. Each subunit has ITS OWN narrow ion pore through the middle of that subunit, drawn as a faint vertical glowing slit. Inside each subunit, a SHORT orange retinal (#FF8A3D) about one third of the subunit width, a small polyene chain with a ring, sitting off to the OUTER side of its subunit next to a helix, NOT crossing the pore. A soft shaft of cyan-blue light comes diagonally from the upper LEFT onto the dimer. Other membrane proteins elsewhere in the bilayer are clearly DIFFERENT: muted grey-lavender (#8A86A8) and dusty rose, never teal. No ions drawn. Generous breathing room, camera slightly low.
